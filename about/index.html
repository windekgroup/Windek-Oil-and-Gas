Our Story

Building Nigerian capability, not importing it

Windek Oil and Gas Limited is a fully indigenous Nigerian energy company headquartered in Port Harcourt, Rivers State. We deliver services across upstream operations, midstream and LPG infrastructure, and engineering and logistics.

Our position as an indigenous operator is deliberate. Nigeria has substantial energy resources and a growing base of local engineering capability, but significant project value has historically left the country through imported expertise. We are building that capability here: developing local project management, engineering and workforce capability on projects measured in years rather than single assets.

That commitment shows up concretely in the way we deliver. We take integrated responsibility as an EPCI contractor rather than bidding only for the packages we can execute, which means more of the project scope, spend and knowledge stays inside the local economy.

  1. Founding

    Established in Rivers State

    Windek Oil and Gas Limited was registered in Nigeria (RC 1493721) and began operations from Port Harcourt, building capability across the oil and gas value chain.

  2. Capability

    Three service lines built out

    Upstream operations, midstream and LPG infrastructure, and engineering and logistics were developed into integrated, accountable service lines rather than separate trade offerings.

  3. Flagship

    LPG Terminal Facility committed

    A 20,000 metric tonne LPG storage and distribution hub at Atabrikang, Akwa Ibom, valued at $100M and delivered as an integrated EPCI project with Cakasa Nigeria Limited.

  4. Next

    Regional expansion

    Expanding LPG infrastructure into additional states while pursuing new exploration and production blocks and developing solar and LPG hybrid solutions.

Who We Are

Operational Excellence
in Energy

Windek delivers energy services from a single indigenous base, with in-house capability across the value chain rather than a network of partner contractors.

That structure decides how a project runs. Engineering, construction, marine logistics and field operations sit under the same management, so scheduling, procurement and site execution are planned by the people who will be accountable for the result. Nigerian content compliance is treated as a delivery requirement on the contract rather than a reporting exercise afterwards.
